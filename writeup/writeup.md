# The Relay: a rate-limited cited-answer pipeline

**1. What I built and why.** Python/asyncio pipeline over the three pinned arXiv papers (1706.03762v7, 2410.00037v1, 2507.07955v2): **retrieve** (BM25 over page-aware pieces; a tool, no tokens) → **gate** (refuse with no model call if the best piece covers <35% of the question's terms) → **generate** (streamed, cites `[n]`, must say "the papers do not say" otherwise) → **verify** (every claim cites an existing passage; one regeneration with more passages if not). Model: `openai/gpt-oss-20b` on Groq's free tier (`llama-3.1-8b-instant` is not on my account). **The decision that shaped it most:** the binding limit is *tokens per minute* (8,000, about 10 answers/min), not requests, so one shared limiter owns it and requests that cannot start within **8 s** are turned away at once, rather than queued to a 20 s deadline. Cost: at 5× overload 78% of people get an instant "no" instead of a slow "yes". Measured gain: served requests' median wait fell from 16.0 s to 6.0 s at the same throughput (40 vs 39 answered).

**2. The real service and my stand-in.** Limits are in Table 1, found by reading `x-ratelimit-*` headers and by hitting limits on purpose (a 40-request burst gave exactly 30 ok then `RPM: Limit 30`; a naive 10-request burst of ~1.4k-token prompts gave `TPM: Limit 8000, Used 7433, Requested 1477`). The stand-in is an SSE server with the same headers and 429 bodies; time-to-first-token (lognormal, median 768 ms, σ 0.34), output length (mean 70 tokens) and refusal rate (19.8%) are fitted to 91 real calls; prompt size had no effect on latency (r = 0.02) so none is modelled. It is **least like the real thing** in the tail and under load: its p99 first token is 1.7 s against 2.75 s real (n = 23, so indicative), and its failure, hang and load-slowdown rates are guesses, because I never pushed the real service into overload. Its answers are canned text, so I only graded quality on real runs.

**3. Results.** Tables 1–3 (last page) are computed from `results.csv` by `relay/analysis/tables.py`; peak queue and offered rate come from `raw/scenarios.jsonl`. Real runs: steady, burst, naive burst, retrieval sweep. Stand-in runs: sustained overload, limit halved, daily budget gone (limits can't be exhausted on the real account). Numbers we could not have guessed: **(a)** median time from first token to last is **21 ms** (n = 101), so first token ≈ total time (ratio 0.97): a reasoning model hides its thinking and streaming buys nothing; **(b)** the daily allowance refills continuously (`reset-requests` = used × 86.4 s), not at midnight, and rejected requests did not count against it (883 → 854 after 30 ok + 10 rejected); **(c)** one answer is charged about 40 more tokens than the API reports (138 vs 96 on a 1-request probe); **(d)** 15 of 15 immediate retries failed. Quality, 23 questions I wrote (18 answerable, 5 not), chosen setting: 60.9% correct and cited, 4.3% wrong, 21.7% correctly refused, 13.0% wrongly refused; 773 tokens per answer (≈ $0.07 per 1,000 answers at list price, notional).

**4. Where the time goes.** Slowest real request (`sweep_A-0018`): 3,222 ms total = 0.2 ms waiting in my queue + 0 ms retrying + 3,193 ms inside the provider call before the first token + 29 ms streaming. So the delay came **before** the first token, and since a typical request of the same size (35 output tokens) is first-token ≈ 790 ms, about 2.4 s was provider-side. I can't split provider queueing from model thinking from the client side. Contrast `burst-0011`: 2,123 ms = 1,200 ms waiting in my queue + 922 ms working. A rate-limited request in the naive burst: 512 ms, all of it 4 rejected attempts.

**5. When a limit is hit.** *Per-minute:* sliding 60 s token and request windows at 90% headroom, FIFO. When committed tokens exceed 70% of the cap (or the queue builds) later arrivals are **degraded** (3→2 passages, ≤250 output tokens, no regeneration); if they still cannot start in 8 s they are **dropped**. Who is delayed: everyone admitted, by ≤8 s. Dropped: the tail of a burst. Weaker answer: 33 of 39 served at 5× overload. Why: tokens are the scarce resource, a smaller context is cheaper than refusing someone, and a quick no beats a slow one. The limiter also shrinks its belief after a 429 (×0.7) so an unannounced halving of both limits cost only 1 of 24 requests a provider rejection (66.7% served, 29.2% gave up waiting). *Per-day:* a daily-limit 429 (or remaining = 0) stops model calls entirely (section 7).

**Retrieval: quality versus cost.** Four settings, same 23 questions (Table 3). Piece size 120 / 3 pieces: 60.9% correct, 773 tokens. 200 / 6 / re-rank: 60.9%, 1,533 tokens. 200 / 4: 57.1% (n = 21; 2 requests lost to a bug I fixed afterwards). 200 / 5 / re-rank / stemming: 56.5%, 8.7% wrong. **I chose 120 / 3 / no re-rank:** equal best quality at half the tokens. Doubling context bought no question; q03, q11 and q14 were refused wrongly at all four settings (q15 failed at three), so the cause is the retriever, not the amount fetched. Near the limit I drop to 2 pieces; if the daily budget is gone I skip the model entirely.

**How this library will mislead a naive retriever.** (1) *Numbers live in table rows, headline numbers in the abstract:* "What BLEU does the **base** Transformer get on WMT14 En-De?" The abstract says 28.4 (that is the *big* model); the right 27.3 is a table row on p.8 that ranked 5th–7th, so every setting refused wrongly. (2) *Lexical mismatch:* "How many parameters does Helium have?" The text says "7B-parameter"; the answer piece ranked 22nd. "Mimi's" never matches "Mimi". Stemming fixed q15 but broke q04 and q16. (3) *One paper dominates and its appendix is noise:* Moshi is 51% of the text and its appendix holds sample conversations, so "What is the capital of France?" scores 5.6 BM25 there and passes a naive score threshold; "How does the Transformer compare to BERT?" fetches Moshi, the only paper that mentions BERT.

**6. Retries.** Up to 2 retries, only for 429, timeout and 5xx; wait = `retry-after` plus jitter; never past the deadline; a daily-limit 429 is not retried. **Where retrying made things worse:** the naive policy (no limiter, 3 immediate retries) on the real service: 5 of 10 requests failed after 4 attempts each within about 0.5 s, though `retry-after` said 7–8 s, so 20 rejected calls for 5 requests. At 5× overload on the stand-in it made 2.58 retries per request and served 15.6% against 21.7% with the limiter.

**7. What the person sees.** At 3 pm with the budget gone, in a median of 4 ms and with no model call: "Today's model capacity is used up, so here are the most relevant passages instead of a written answer:" plus the top 3 passages with paper, version and page. Half of those (5 of 10) contained the answer. Known gap: I flag the budget as gone and never clear it, but the real allowance refills about every 86 s per request.

**8. Unsure, left out, learned.** *Least certain:* that the 8 s patience matches how long people really wait (I did not measure it) and that hidden reasoning tokens are about 40 per call (one data point); if either is wrong the limiter over- or under-admits, and the 429 feedback is my only protection. *Not built:* dense/embedding retrieval or an LLM re-ranker (it would spend the scarce tokens and add failure modes); priority classes and answer caching. Also not measured: the real tokens-per-day limit. *New to me:* in a sliding-window limit, a rejected request needs `Requested − (Limit − Used)` tokens to age out, here 1,477 − 567 = 910 tokens, which is the 6.8 s the server told me; retrying sooner cannot succeed.

**Your feedback on Vaani.** The page's strongest claim is latency ("sub-500 ms", "~450 ms p90"), but it never says what is timed (end of speech to first audio?), at which load, or why both figures appear. A text-only fetch showed the counters as 0 (likely animation; not checked in a browser). I would define the latency number on the page, because it is what a buyer will test.

**How I worked with the assistant.** Full raw session: `history/conversation.md` (my API key redacted). *Prompts that mattered:* "make a plan and set up the project layout"; pasting my Groq key so it could probe the real service; "use auto mode, complete it". *What it got wrong, caught by its own measurements:* it graded three correct answers wrong (the model wrote `3.5 days` with a non-breaking space and cited as `【1】`), which also made the pipeline regenerate and then throw away good answers; it first queued every request up to a 20 s deadline (median first token 16.9 s under overload). *Decision overruled:* none in this session; I accepted its design and measurements. *What is mine:* the framing and the go-ahead only. The code, the 23 questions, the 120 / 3 choice and the 8 s patience were written or proposed by the assistant, so I must be able to explain and change each of them live.
